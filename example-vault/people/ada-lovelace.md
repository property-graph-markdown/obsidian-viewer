# Ada Lovelace

Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, lived from 1815 to 1852. She was a mathematician and writer known for her work on Charles Babbage's proposed Analytical Engine.[^ada-overview]

## Family

Her father was [Lord Byron](lord-byron.md "{type: has_father}").[^ada-overview]

Her mother was [Anne Isabella Milbanke](anne-isabella-milbanke.md "{type: has_mother}").[^ada-overview]

She married [William King-Noel](william-king-noel.md "{type: married, year: 1835}") in 1835.[^ada-adult-years]

Ada and William had three children: [Byron King-Noel](byron-king-noel.md "{type: parent_of, birth_order: 1}"), born in 1836; [Anne Blunt](anne-blunt.md "{type: parent_of, birth_order: 2}"), born in 1837; and [Ralph King-Milbanke](ralph-king-milbanke.md "{type: parent_of, birth_order: 3}"), born in 1839.[^ada-adult-years]

## Study and collaboration

[Mary Somerville](mary-somerville.md "{type: taught_by}") taught Ada, corresponded with her, and introduced her to Charles Babbage in 1833.[^ada-adult-years]  [Mary Somerville](mary-somerville.md "{type: introduced_by, year: 1833, introduced_to: \"Charles Babbage\"}")

Ada and [Charles Babbage](charles-babbage.md "{type: collaborated_with, from: 1833, topic: \"Analytical Engine\"}") developed a long friendship and working relationship around his calculating machines from 1833.[^ada-overview]

[Augustus De Morgan](augustus-de-morgan.md "{type: taught_by, period: \"1840s\", topic: \"advanced mathematics\"}") helped her study advanced mathematics, including Bernoulli numbers, during the 1840s.[^ada-education]

During 1842–1843, Ada translated a French article by [Luigi Menabrea](luigi-menabrea.md "{type: translated_work_by, from: 1842, to: 1843}") into English and added seven Notes.[^ada-notes]

[Charles Wheatstone](charles-wheatstone.md "{type: commissioned_by, year: 1842, work: \"Menabrea translation\"}") prompted that translation.[^ada-notes]

## Work

In 1842–1843, Ada helped explain and communicate the [Analytical Engine](../work/analytical-engine.md "{type: contributed_to, from: 1842, to: 1843, role: \"analysis and exposition\"}") and described its potential beyond numerical calculation.[^ada-notes]

Her [translation and Notes A–G](../work/menabrea-translation-and-notes.md "{type: published, year: 1843, role: \"translator and annotator\"}"), published in 1843, contain the detailed Bernoulli-number procedure preserved as [Note G](../work/note-g.md "{type: contributed_to, year: 1843, role: \"annotator\"}"). The widespread label “first programmer” stems from this publication, but remains contested because Babbage had earlier unpublished programs and contributed to the algorithm.[^ada-program]

## Places

Ada was born in [London](../places/london.md "{type: born_in, year: 1815}").[^ada-overview]

After her marriage, she lived at family residences in [Surrey](../places/surrey.md "{type: lived_in, from: 1835}").[^ada-adult-years]

From 1845, [Horsley Towers](../places/horsley-towers.md "{type: lived_at, from: 1845, to: 1852}") was the family's principal home.[^ada-adult-years]

She died in Marylebone, London, in 1852.[^ada-overview] [London](../places/london.md "{type: died_in, year: 1852, district: \"Marylebone\"}")

## Legacy

The later [Ada programming language](../work/ada-programming-language.md "{type: namesake_of, named: 1979}") was named in her honor.[^ada-overview]
